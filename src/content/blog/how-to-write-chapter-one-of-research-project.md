Standard 5-Step Structure for Writing the Background to the Study

A well-written Background to the Study should follow a logical progression from a broad discussion of the research topic to the specific problem being investigated. The purpose is to provide sufficient context, demonstrate the significance of the issue, identify existing knowledge, reveal gaps in the literature, and justify the need for the study.

1. Global Context

Begin by presenting a broad overview of the topic from an international perspective. Introduce the major concepts, variables, or issues related to the study and explain their significance within the wider global context. Where appropriate, include historical developments, international trends, and relevant statistics to demonstrate the importance of the phenomenon being investigated. This section establishes the foundation upon which the rest of the background is built.

2. Regional or Local Context

After discussing the global situation, gradually narrow the focus to the region, country, state, institution, or community where the study is situated. Explain how the issue manifests within the specific geographical or social setting of the research. Support the discussion with recent data obtained from government publications, institutional reports, policy documents, or other credible sources. This transition helps readers understand the relevance of the problem within the study area.

3. Current State of Knowledge

Provide a concise synthesis of existing knowledge on the topic. Rather than discussing individual studies separately, organize the literature around major themes, patterns, trends, or perspectives that have emerged from previous research. Highlight important findings, theoretical explanations, and established relationships among the study variables. The objective is to show what is currently known about the issue without turning the section into a full literature review.

4. Identification of the Research Gap

Clearly indicate the limitations, inconsistencies, or unanswered questions that exist within the current body of knowledge. The research gap may arise from conflicting findings, methodological weaknesses, inadequate geographical coverage, outdated evidence, neglected populations, or insufficient attention to particular variables. This section should explicitly demonstrate that despite previous research efforts, important aspects of the problem remain unresolved. Statements such as “however, little is known about…”, “previous studies have largely overlooked…”, or “existing evidence remains inconclusive regarding…” are useful for emphasizing the gap.

5. Study Rationale and Purpose

Conclude the background by explaining why the present study is necessary. Show how the research is positioned to address the identified gap and contribute to knowledge, policy, practice, or theory. Establish a clear connection between the problem identified and the proposed investigation. The final paragraph should naturally lead into the purpose of the study by stating the overall objective or central focus of the research.

Step-by-Step Guide for Writing the Background to the Study
Step 1: Introduce the Topic Broadly

Start with a general discussion of the study variables or concepts. Explain their significance and relevance within the broader field of study. Where necessary, provide concise definitions of specialized concepts and support the discussion with global statistics, trends, or evidence that demonstrate the importance of the issue.

Step 2: Narrow the Discussion to the Study Setting

Move gradually from the international perspective to the specific context in which the study is conducted. Discuss how the issue affects the target population, geographical area, institution, or community. Use recent and credible local evidence to establish the practical relevance of the study.

Step 3: Summarize Existing Knowledge

Briefly review what previous studies have revealed about the topic. Focus on major themes and consistent findings rather than providing lengthy descriptions of individual studies. The aim is to show the current state of knowledge and create a foundation for identifying unresolved issues.

Step 4: Establish the Research Gap

Identify what previous studies have not adequately addressed. Point out areas where evidence is lacking, inconsistent, outdated, or contextually limited. The gap should emerge logically from the preceding discussion and clearly demonstrate the need for further investigation.

Step 5: Justify the Study

Explain how the current study will contribute to addressing the identified gap. Describe its relevance and expected contribution to knowledge or practice. End by stating the main objective of the research, thereby providing a smooth transition to the next section of the study.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

One of the most common errors in academic writing is treating the Background to the Study as a Literature Review. While both sections draw on existing research, the background provides context and justification for the study, whereas the literature review offers a more detailed analysis and evaluation of previous studies.

Another frequent mistake is making unsupported claims. All factual statements, statistics, historical information, and trend analyses should be supported with credible and recent references.

Writers should also avoid excessive technical language that may reduce clarity. The background should be understandable to readers from different academic backgrounds while maintaining scholarly standards.

Finally, attention should be paid to tense usage. Previous studies, historical events, and completed investigations should generally be discussed in the past tense, while statements concerning the purpose and objectives of the current study should be written in the present tense.

A strong Background to the Study therefore follows a clear progression: global context → local context → existing knowledge → research gap → study rationale and purpose. This structure ensures coherence, logical flow, and a convincing justification for the research.
