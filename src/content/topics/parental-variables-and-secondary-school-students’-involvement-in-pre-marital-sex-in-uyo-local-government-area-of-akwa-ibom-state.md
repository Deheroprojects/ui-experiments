abstract: >
  This research investigated the relationship between parental variables and
  secondary school students’ involvement in pre-marital sex in Uyo Local
  Government Area of Akwa Ibom State

  . The study examined five key factors: parents’ income, educational attainment, sexual communication, supervision, and family structure

  .

  Using a descriptive survey research design, the study focused on a population of 6,083 senior secondary three (SS3) students

  . A sample of 100 students was selected through stratified random sampling, and data were gathered using a structured questionnaire titled “Parental Variables and Secondary School Students’ Involvement in Pre-marital Sex Questionnaire (PVSSSIPSQ)”

  . The data were analyzed using the Pearson Product Moment Correlation Coefficient

  .

  The findings revealed significant relationships between all studied parental variables and students' involvement in pre-marital sex

  . Specifically:

  Parental sexual communication (r=0.65) and parents' income (r=0.57) showed the strongest positive relationships

  .

  Parents' educational attainment (r=0.54) had a moderate positive relationship

  .

  Parental supervision and family structure (r=0.25) showed weaker but still significant positive relationships

  .

  The study concluded that better financial resources and frequent discussions about sexual matters are associated with higher student involvement in pre-marital sex

  . Based on these results, the research recommended that government agencies and NGOs organize parental seminars, schools implement comprehensive sex education, and family counseling services be made available to help parents frame sexual health discussions within a moral and responsible context

CHAPTER ONE
INTRODUCTION
1.1 	Background of the Study
The period of adolescence is characterized by a transition from childhood to adulthood, marked by physical, emotional, and cognitive and often associated with heightened risk-taking, including premarital sex development (Aleke, Obiekwe, and Umebali 2021). The transitional period can bring up issues of independence and self-identity; many adolescents and their peers face tough choices regarding schoolwork, sexuality, drugs, alcohol and social life. Peer groups, romantic interests and external appearance tend to naturally increase in importance for some time during a teen’s journey toward adulthood. While adolescence is a time of tremendous growth and potential, it is also a time of considerable risk during which social contexts exert powerful influences. Many adolescents face pressures to use alcohol, cigarettes, or other drugs and to initiate sexual relationships at earlier ages, putting themselves at high risk for intentional and unintentional injuries, unintended pregnancies and infection from sexually transmitted infections (STIs), including the human immunodeficiency virus (HIV). Many also experience a wide range of adjustment and mental health problems. Behaviour patterns that are established during this process, such as drug use or non-use and sexual risk taking or protection, can have long-lasting positive and negative effects on future health and well-being. As a result, during this process, adults have unique opportunities to influence young people (Bachman, O'Malley, and Schulenberg, 2020).
Premarital sexual activity is a common sexual experimentation among adolescents which usually involves behaviours such as multiple sexual partners inconsistent or non-condom use and consumption of pornography (United Nations Agency for International Development [UNAIDS, 2015). Many youths have engaged themselves in premarital sex as a result of curiosity and the pleasure derived from it. The youth thought they were searching for reality, but they ended up destroying themselves. They are not satisfied with what their parents, pastors and Christian friends told them concerning sex, they want to experience it themselves (Adewale, 2018). The rate of teenage pregnancy and the high level of indulgence in premarital sex among students in this 21st century have become subjects of discourse (Ogbueghu, 2017).  
Historically, premarital sex was considered a moral issue which was taboo in many cultures and considered a sin by a number of religions, but since about the 1960s, it has become more widely accepted, especially in Western countries. In some cultures, for example in many modern-day Western cultures, many people do not hold value in sexual abstinence before marriage. In traditional Nigerian society, strict moral principles, culture, religious and social influences were regarded as restrictions to socially and culturally unacceptable sexual behaviours (Eze, 2014). These restrictions and consequences created a culture of silence and prevented open discussion of sexuality between adolescents and well-informed adults. However, the increasing exposure to the internet and social media, and the consequential adoption of foreign ways of life by young people, have paved the way for more tolerance for sexual lifestyles that were previously regarded as offensive or socially unacceptable (Ajaegbu, 2015; Olumide and Ojengbede, 2016). 
In many societies, the issue of adolescent pre-marital sex is compounded by socio-cultural norms and family dynamics. These factors significantly influence adolescents' attitudes and behaviors towards sex. In particular, parental involvement plays a crucial role in shaping students involvement in premarital sex. Parental variables, such as involvement in children's lives, family structure, sexual communication  about sexual matters, and socio-cultural norms, can either mitigate or exacerbate risky sexual behaviors among secondary school students (Oladipupo-Okorie & Viatonu, 2014).
Some researcher found that students have been observed engaging in sexual activities without proper education, and as such, they are exposed and induced to unfamiliar risks and Sexually Transmitted Diseases [STDs] such as gonorrhea, syphilis, candidiasis, chlamydia, and Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome [AIDS] (Ezeugwu and Ede, 2016). The recent Nigerian Demographic and Health Survey ([NDHS], 2019) report shows that 7.9% of boys and 9.6% of girls aged 15–19 years had sexual intercourse with persons who were neither their husbands nor lived with them. The Federal Ministry of Health ([FMoH], 2013) survey on the sexual behaviours of Nigerians revealed that almost half of the females (46.2%) and about a quarter of males (22.1%) between the ages of 15 and 19 years have engaged in sexual inter- course.
According to Adeoye, Ola and Aliu; (2015), modern day youths are more susceptible to the dangers of illicit sex than previous generations. Unfortunately, some Nigerian youths actively engaged in premarital sex despite the detrimental consequences associated with it. Abdullahi (2013) and Ekpenyong (2016) strongly believe that premarital sex poses a lot of health challenges to those who engage in it. Students who engage in premarital sex run the risk of contracting Sexually Transmitted Infections (STI) such as AIDs, HIV, Chlamydia, Gonorrhea, Genital herpes, general Warts, Trichomoniasis, syphilis and so on. These diseases are life-threatening, painful and can cause infertility. Students who engage in group sex, sharp-sharp sex and sodomy account for a greater proportion of STI’s victims, unwanted pregnancy, school dropout, victims of rituals, depression, poor academic performance, loss of respect, feeling of regret and guilt and so on.
Several studies have explored the practice of premarital sexual activities among adolescents; however, none of these studies have focused parental factors as predictors of premarital sexual activities among adolescent students in Uyo, Akwa Ibom State, Nigeria. This study, therefore, aims at examining parental variables and secondary school students’ involvement in premarital sex in Uyo. 
1.2 	Statement of the Problem
Adolescence is a critical period characterized by significant physical, emotional, and cognitive changes. This transitional phase from childhood to adulthood often involves increased risk-taking behaviors, including engagement in premarital sex. Students involvement in premarital sex can be influenced by a variety of factors, including peer pressure, romantic interests, and socio-cultural norms. In Uyo Local Government Area, there is a growing concern about the high rates of premarital sex among secondary school students, which is associated with adverse outcomes such as unintended pregnancies, sexually transmitted infections (STIs), and other related health and social issues.
Despite various studies exploring the prevalence and consequences of premarital sexual activities among adolescents, there is a lack of focused research on the role of parental variables in predicting these behaviors, particularly within the context of Uyo. Parental involvement, family structure, and parental sexual communication about sexual matters are critical factors that can either mitigate or exacerbate the likelihood of adolescents engaging in premarital sex.
This study seeks to address this gap by examining how parental variables influence secondary school students' involvement in premarital sex in Uyo. Understanding these relationships is essential for developing targeted interventions that can help reduce the prevalence of risky sexual behaviors among adolescents and promote healthier outcomes. By investigating the impact of parental involvement, family type, and sexual communication about sexual issues, this research aims to provide valuable insights that can inform education policy makers, teachers, healthcare workers, and non-governmental organizations. These stakeholders can utilize the findings to formulate effective reproductive and sexual health policies, improve classroom instruction and counseling, and enhance sexual and reproductive health services for adolescents. Ultimately, this study seeks to contribute to a better understanding of the factors influencing students’ involvement in premarital sex in Uyo, thereby filling a critical gap in empirical data and paving the way for future research in this area.
